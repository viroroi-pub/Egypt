edge_ramp_montecarlo_10000x5.CSV:

Method:
The dataset was created using a Monte Carlo simulation of the adaptive edge-ramp model. Each trial propagated uncertainty in the key logistical parameters:

hauling speed varied by ±25% around the baseline,

block dispatch headway varied by ±1 minute,

additional stochastic delays were added at corners, modeled as log-normal with median ≈ 2.8 minutes and σ ≈ 0.35.

The simulation engine generated 10,000 independent trials for each of the five construction phases (16-ramp, 8-ramp, 4-ramp, 2-ramp, and 1-ramp), yielding the 50,000-row CSV table. Each row reports ramp capacity, queue length, waiting probability, and blocking probability for one simulated trial.

Random seed:
The Monte Carlo generator was initialized with a fixed seed = 42 (Python numpy.random.default_rng(42)), ensuring reproducibility of the dataset.

montecarlo_edge_ramp_trials.csv:

Method used

A discrete-event simulation of block hauling on the Integrated Edge-Ramp (IER) system.

Each ramp was discretized into cells; one team per cell to maintain safety spacing.

Baseline speeds: 0.15 m/s on ramps, 0.20 m/s on terraces.

Baseline dispatch headway: 4 minutes per ramp (with scenarios tested at 3–8 minutes).

Corner maneuver times modeled as lognormal delays (median ≈ 2.8 min, σ = 0.35), reflecting lever-and-drag experiments.

Stochasticity introduced as:

±25% hauling speed variation

±1 minute headway variation

Occasional random stoppages (MTBF ≈ 6 h; repair 5–12 min).

Monte Carlo sampling: 10,000 trials per phase (16 → 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 ramps), giving ≈ 50,000 rows total.

Random seed

To ensure reproducibility, the runs were initialized with a fixed Python RNG seed = 42 (numpy.random.default_rng(seed=42)).

This guarantees that the CSV can be regenerated identically from the archived code.

montecarlo_edge_ramp_trials_mu015_mu030_theta6_7_8.zip 

It was generated using a Monte Carlo discrete-event simulation of block flows along the Integrated Edge-Ramp system.

Method:

A recursive parametric algorithm (Unity/C# with Python analysis scripts) modeled ramp geometry and logistics.

Each trial simulated hauling speeds (±25%), dispatch headways (±1 minute), and corner turning delays (lognormal distribution) across the adaptive 16→8→4→2→1 ramp sequence.

Output metrics included ramp capacity (blocks/hour), mean corner queue length, waiting probability, blocking probability, and total on-site duration.

N = 10,000 trials per parameter set, covering friction coefficients μ = 0.15–0.30 and ramp slopes θ = 6–8°.

Seed:
The simulations used a fixed random seed = 42 (Python numpy.random.seed(42)) to ensure full reproducibility of the stochastic outputs archived on Zenodo.

So the dataset represents 10,000 reproducible stochastic trials per scenario, with random variability in haul speeds, headways, and corner delays, controlled by seed 42.

edge_ramp_montecarlo_byphase_Sadjusted_ALL.zip:

Method

A discrete-event, stochastic simulation was run to model block transport through the integrated edge-ramp system.

Each phase of the adaptive sequence (16 → 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 ramps) was simulated separately.

The simulation parameters incorporated uncertainty in block hauling speed (±25%), dispatch headway (±1 minute), and corner-turn delays (modeled as lognormal distributions).

10,000 Monte Carlo trials per phase were run, producing distributions of ramp capacity (blocks/hour), corner queue lengths, waiting probabilities, and blocking probabilities.

Results were consolidated into CSV tables, and the bundled .zip file includes all per-phase stochastic outputs for reproducibility.

Seed

The Monte Carlo trials were initialized with a fixed random seed = 42, ensuring full reproducibility of all results in the Zenodo dataset.
